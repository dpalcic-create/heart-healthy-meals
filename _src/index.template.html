Welcome, Dave. Every recipe stays inside your approved foods — gluten-free, dairy-free, no added sugar, and built for a heart-healthy pattern. Plan a week, auto-fill if you want, then use the shopping list. This is not medical advice; follow your doctor’s guidance.

Gentle daily targets: protein at least 90g · saturated fat under 15g (amber to 20g) · sodium under 1,500mg (amber to 2,300mg). Totals are estimates from the meals you plan, including a snack or treat if one is on the day. Recipes you can make

Approved foods

This is the list the recipes are built from, aligned with a gluten-free, sugar-free, dairy-free, low-processed, high-protein pattern and a high coronary calcium (CAC) score. The focus is LDL control, blood pressure, and overall cardiovascular health.

Proteins (primary focus)

  • Chicken, skinless breast or thigh
  • Turkey (fresh or ground, not deli meat)
  • Salmon, rainbow trout, and other fatty fish
  • White fish such as cod and haddock
  • Shrimp and other plain shellfish
  • Lean beef or bison, occasional and in modest portions
  • Pork tenderloin or other lean cuts
  • Previnex shakes or a similar clean, unsweetened protein powder you already tolerate
  • Plain peanuts or natural peanut butter, unsalted, no added sugar

Vegetables

  • Brussels sprouts, broccoli, cauliflower
  • Spinach, kale, and other leafy greens
  • Asparagus, green beans, zucchini, cucumber
  • Bell peppers, onions, garlic, mushrooms, cabbage

Healthy fats

  • Olive oil
  • Avocado, plain, in measured portions
  • Fatty fish, listed above
  • Plain nuts: almonds, walnuts, macadamia, peanuts — about 1 ounce, or ½ ounce for macadamias
  • Natural nut butters with no added sugar or oils, measured tablespoons

Drinks

  • Plain water, sparkling water, black coffee, unsweetened tea
  • Occasional bourbon or dry wine, limited, as previously discussed with your clinician

Seasonings used in this app

  • Herbs and spices, unlimited — including garlic and onion as vegetables, plus dried spices, fresh herbs, and fresh ginger
  • Salt, in moderation
  • Lemon juice, lime juice, and unsweetened vinegar, used as seasonings
  • Pure vanilla extract, in a few desserts
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder, optional and clearly marked, desserts only

Foods to minimize or avoid

  • Anything with gluten — no bread, pasta, flour, oats, or other grains
  • Added sugars or sweetened products, including honey, maple, syrups, and non-sugar sweeteners
  • Dairy: milk, cheese, yogurt, butter, cream, and the rest
  • Eggs, and fruit other than avocado (they are not on your approved list, so they stay out of the recipes)
  • Legumes other than peanuts — no beans, lentils, or chickpeas
  • Highly processed meats or packaged foods, including deli turkey, bacon, and sausage
  • Fried foods — sautéing in a teaspoon or two of olive oil is not the same as frying
  • Excess saturated fat from fatty cuts of meat
  • Prepared sauces and condiments that often hide sugar or gluten (soy sauce, ketchup, bottled mustard, mayo)

Portions of nuts and any higher-fat proteins stay moderate so overall calorie and saturated-fat intake remains controlled.

Cooking methods

Stay with bake, grill, roast, broil, steam, parchment packets, and skillet sauté in a measured amount of olive oil. An air fryer is fine when you use it like a small roaster — a light brush of oil, not a bath of oil, and never a deep fry.

Low-sodium flavor

  • Lemon or lime at the end of cooking brightens food more than another pinch of salt.
  • Unsweetened vinegar in a cabbage slaw or over fish does the same job.
  • Garlic, onion, smoked paprika, cumin, dill, rosemary, thyme, and crushed red pepper carry a lot of flavor.
  • Toast spices briefly in the pan. Let mushrooms and onions brown instead of salting early.
  • Shrimp, scallops, and some protein powders already bring sodium. Taste before you add salt.

Portions and rhythm

  • Nuts: about 1 ounce is a small handful, or roughly 23 almonds. Measure, don’t eat from the jar.
  • Macadamias are higher in saturated fat — keep those closer to ½ ounce (about 5 or 6 nuts).
  • Nut butters: 1 tablespoon is a typical snack portion.
  • Avocado: a half, or a quarter when the plate already has salmon or nut butter.
  • Lean beef or bison: modest, about 4 ounces cooked, and only 1–2 times a week. Auto-fill follows that rule.
  • Fatty fish (salmon or trout): aim for at least two meals a week; auto-fill aims for three.
  • Desserts in this app are treat-style, not sweet. They use nuts, spices, protein powder, and sometimes unsweetened cocoa. There is no added sugar and no sweetener.

Alcohol

Occasional bourbon or dry wine, limited, as you and your clinician have already discussed. This app does not put alcohol into recipes. If you have been advised to skip it, skip it.

Meal prep

Recipes tagged meal-prep keep well for 2–3 days. Roast a tray of chicken or a pork tenderloin on Sunday, bake an extra salmon fillet, and cook a batch of turkey patties. Leftover notes are on every recipe. Reheat gently; don’t scorch leftover fish or shrimp.

A short disclaimer

Follow the plan your doctor has given you, including any limits on sodium, protein, potassium, or alcohol, and get help if a food on this list disagrees with you.
